# OLC Learning Lab: Reading a Bison Habitat Suitability Map

## Purpose
Use this series to practice spatial reasoning, data literacy, and thoughtful
questions about bison restoration. The map is a conversation starter—not a
decision about where bison belong.

## Suggested learning questions

1. Which components create the strongest pattern in the combined BHSI map?
2. How does a map change when vegetation or water is given more importance?
3. Where do satellite classifications disagree with what people know from the land?
4. What data would help distinguish reliable water from seasonal water?
5. How could climate, fencing, roads, access, and seasonal movement change an
   interpretation of a high-score patch?

## Activities

### 1. Map observation (30 minutes)
Open the dashboard. Choose each layer, record two observations, one question,
and one possible limitation. Compare observations with a partner.

### 2. Model critique (45 minutes)
Read `documents/bhsi_weights.md`. Propose a different set of weights and explain
whose knowledge or management goal motivated each change. Discuss why a change
in weights is not automatically a change in truth.

### 3. Ground-truth design (60 minutes)
Choose a candidate area only after appropriate review. Design a field-observation
sheet for vegetation, water reliability, slope, signs of use, and questions for
local knowledge holders. Identify what must not be recorded or shared publicly.

## Further study ideas

- Compare the SRTM topography layer with local field observations.
- Build seasonal water-access and forage-condition layers.
- Evaluate how different climate scenarios change planning assumptions.
- Study uncertainty: where do data gaps or classification errors matter most?
- Develop community-approved indicators that complement federal datasets.

## Respectful use
Follow `documents/data_sovereignty.md`. Any external sharing or use of specific
candidate locations requires the review and authorization described there.
