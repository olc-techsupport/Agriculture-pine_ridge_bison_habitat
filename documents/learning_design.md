# Learning design and outcomes

## Series outcomes

Learners will be able to:

1. Use an ordered, reproducible geospatial workflow.
2. Trace a mapped result from public source through configuration, transformation, and output.
3. Evaluate scale, resolution, classification, missingness, and spatial variation.
4. Explain how weights, thresholds, and minimum patch area encode assumptions.
5. Compare model scenarios without equating robustness with truth.
6. Separate observation, interpretation, additional evidence, and authorized decision.
7. Explain why public discoverability does not remove Indigenous data-governance responsibilities.
8. Make and review a small reproducibility improvement.

## Instructional contract

The series uses public data for learning and screening. It does not contain OST-controlled data, establish OLC or OST findings, identify approved restoration priorities, or replace knowledge held by Oglala Lakota people and authorized programs. Cultural, ecological, biological, and management claims require appropriate sources and local review.

## Notebook requirements

Every notebook includes objectives, prerequisites and timing, a governance checkpoint, a learner checkpoint, an interpretation protocol, a contribution activity, and a connection to the next module. Automated tests protect the presence of this structure but do not substitute for learner pilots or OLC/OST review.

## Assessment

Learners produce a short evidence record naming one regenerated result, its source and spatial/temporal scope, one transformation, one assumption, one limitation, one alternative explanation, and one question requiring local knowledge or additional evidence.

## Ownership of instructional development

These learning sections are scaffolding for adaptation. Daear Consulting LLC developed the geospatial code, workflows, and documentation under contract to Oglala Lakota College. OLC will develop the full instructional materials, facilitation choices, and curriculum used with its learners.
